01

How the voice is made

Four steps, several centuries apart

  1. 文

    Translate

    Your English, Hindi, or Telugu text is translated into Classical Sanskrit, using a live translation when available or a phrasebook match when not.

  2. अ

    Transliterate

    The Sanskrit is read as Devanagari and IAST, so the script and its Latin transliteration can be considered together.

  3. ॒

    Apply accent rules

    Known lexical accents and a set of Pāṇinian rules estimate udātta, anudātta, and svarita pitch. A rule-based estimate is not the same as a recovered original recitation.

  4. ♫

    Speak

    A clear modern neural voice reads the Sanskrit, and the accent is laid over it as a gentle pitch contour. It is generated sound, not a preserved performance tradition.

02

What this cannot tell you

A reconstruction has edges

Nobody has a recording of Vedic Sanskrit.

This page combines a modern translation into Classical Sanskrit, an accent analysis, and a synthetic voice. The input is not transformed into a historically verified Vedic sentence. Translation can be approximate; accent can be uncertain; pitch and timbre are not an ancient recording.

Likewise, the Indus Lab measures distributions and sequences in inscriptions. Statistical regularities can tell us about structure, but not establish what a sign means or prove that a proposed decipherment is right.

What powers it

  • Translation: Google Translate's public endpoint (English, Hindi or Telugu into Classical Sanskrit), called live; a built-in phrasebook takes over if it is unreachable. Machine translation can be wrong.
  • Accent: 20,201 accented headwords from Monier-Williams (Cologne digital edition) plus Pāṇini's tone rules 1.2.29–31, 1.2.39–40, 8.4.66 and 8.1.28. Rigveda lines in the phrasebook keep their traditional accent marks. Words not in the lexicon get a first-syllable guess, shown as “accent by rule”.
  • Voice, phrasebook and Rigveda lines: pre-recorded with a neural text-to-speech voice reading the Devanagari slowly, as Sanskrit. The Vedic accent is then applied as a gentle pitch contour (about ±1.5 semitones, smooth glides; udātta raised, anudātta lowered, svarita falling) with Praat's PSOLA resynthesis. Syllable timings come from forced alignment (Meta's MMS aligner), so the highlight and the pitch cursor follow the audio itself. The curve in the chart is the measured pitch of the recording.
  • Voice, your own words: Google's Hindi text-to-speech reads the Sanskrit live. Syllable timing is measured from the audio in your browser (in our test it landed within about 0.03 s of a forced aligner), and the same gentle contour is applied there.
  • Room echo and drone are optional and off by default.
  • Indus Lab data: 2,423 inscriptions from the open indus-website database (Yajnadevam, GPL-3.0, ICIT numbering), sign concordance from mayig/indus-valley-script-corpus (MIT), sign drawings made for this site, seal photos from Wikimedia Commons (public domain / CC0 / CC BY-SA, credited on each card).
